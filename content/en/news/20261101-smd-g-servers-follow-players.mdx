During the last tests, players from other continents suffered from a lot of latency. So we designed the next step of our server meshing: **Server Meshing Dynamic Gravity**, or **SMD-G**.

The idea: **servers "fall" towards the players**. The part of the universe around a group of players can be simulated in the datacenter closest to most of them, while keeping **one single universe shared by everyone**.

⚠️ This is still a **design**: nothing is coded yet. The numbers below are targets, and they will be measured during the tests.

## 🧱 Preamble: what we already have

Today, the game uses **Server Meshing Dynamic (SMD)**. The universe is split into **zones**, and each zone is simulated by a Godot game server. **Horizon**, our central server, is the conductor:

- **At startup**, a single Godot server handles space and the planets.
- **Split**: when there are too many players or the server slows down, the zone is cut in two and a new server takes one half. The cut avoids going through the middle of a village.
- **Handover**: when you cross the border between two zones, you move from one server to the other, along with what you carry or transport.
- **Merge**: when the load drops, the zones are joined again and the extra server is released.

![Server Meshing Dynamic: start, split, then handover and merge](/assets/images/news/20261101-smdg-smd-today-en.png)

It works well, and the load tests proved it. But **everything runs in Paris** (actually in Propières, France, for now).

## 🐢 The problem: distance

DyingStar is an **authoritative** game: your client guesses nothing, it waits for the server to send your new position. Every step makes the round trip to the server.

For a player in Montreal, that means crossing the Atlantic twice on every step, about **95 ms of network**. If the space around them were simulated in Montreal, it would be about **10 ms**.

![Today, the input crosses the Atlantic; with a bubble handed to Montreal, everything stays local](/assets/images/news/20261101-smdg-latency-en.png)

From click to screen, including the server's processing time, we go from about **130 to 140 ms** down to about **40 to 50 ms**.

Just moving the connection closer would not be enough: as long as the simulation stays in Paris, the trip stays the same. **The space around the players has to be simulated close to them.**

## 🌍 SMD-G: several datacenters, one universe

In this design, there are three sites: **Paris**, **Montreal** and **Hong Kong**. Two words to keep apart:

- **Zone**: the geographic area of a Godot server in Paris, as today with SMD.
- **Bubble**: a part of the universe that Paris delegates to another datacenter, where it is simulated.

And each site has its role:

- **Paris stays the core.** Its servers simulate the zones. It decides which bubbles to hand to which site, handles accounts and the universe's save, and keeps a copy of everything.
- **Montreal and Hong Kong are edges.** They welcome the players of their region and run their own Godot servers for the bubbles handed to them.
- **Everyone stays in the same universe.** There is no separate "US" or "Asia" server: a player in Montreal can join a friend in Paris at any time.

![Architecture: a core in Paris, edges in Montreal and Hong Kong](/assets/images/news/20261101-smdg-architecture-en.png)

If an edge is unavailable, everything falls back to Paris, like today. Handing a bubble to another site is an optimization, never an obligation.

## 🎮 In practice, for you as a player

### 🔌 When you log in

- The game measures your ping to each site and connects you to the fastest one. That is your **home site** for the whole session.
- If nobody from another continent is close to your spawn point, a bubble is **created directly around you on your site**. You arrive without any switch.
- If your site has no server available (at night, for example), you play in a Paris zone, and your bubble is created as soon as a server is ready.

### 🫧 Alone or in a small group: the bubble

Players explore, go on missions and move their habitation: after a few hours, everyone is scattered. So SMD-G keeps following your real positions.

- **Bubble**: a Montreal player alone, far from any Parisian, gets a bubble around them, simulated in Montreal.
- **Moving border**: when they get close to the edge of their bubble and nobody is waiting on the other side, the edge moves with them, instead of making them change server.
- **Pre-emptive merge**: when two groups from different continents get closer (less than 2 km on foot), they are gathered on a single site **before** they meet.

![The three mechanisms: bubble, moving border and pre-emptive merge](/assets/images/news/20261101-smdg-bubbles-en.png)

Why the pre-emptive merge? Two players on two different servers cannot interact directly. That is already the case today. So SMD-G makes sure a border between sites **never runs through a group**, a fight or a trade.

Distances adapt to speed: on foot, a few hundred meters of margin are enough. In a fast spaceship, the margin is counted in tens of kilometers.

### ⚖️ Across continents: the bubble goes where most people play

When players from different regions play in the same place, the core computes every 5 seconds which site gives them **the lowest total latency**.

![Example: 3 Montreal players and 1 from Paris, the group moves into a bubble in Montreal](/assets/images/news/20261101-smdg-score-en.png)

- **3 Montreal players and 1 Parisian**: the group moves into a bubble in Montreal. The Parisian gets about 90 ms, that is the price of being the minority.
- **2 vs 2**: it's a tie, nothing moves.
- **Three continents**: Paris often wins, because it sits in the middle.
- **Fairness cap**: if another option exists, no player should go above 180 ms.
- **A ship's pilot counts double**, an AFK player barely counts, and NPCs don't count.
- **A big event** (100 players from everywhere) stays on a single site, the best one for the whole crowd. It is then spread across several servers of that site.

And no cheating: inflating your ping or switching VPN mid-game does not let you pull a bubble away from your opponents.

### 🔄 When you change site

- The new site **prepares the bubble in the background** while you keep playing normally.
- Then the bubble is frozen for about **150 to 250 ms**, the time to send the final state. No reconnection, nothing lost, and no action is played twice.
- No ping-pong: a bubble only changes site if the gain is clear (at least 25 ms per player) and lasts at least 30 seconds. It then stays put for at least 3 minutes.
- If you cross a border between two sites (leaving your bubble for a Paris zone, for example), you are frozen for about 50 ms.

### 🛟 What about outages?

- The golden rule: **never two servers writing the same part of the universe at the same time**. No item duplication, even if the network breaks.
- If Montreal loses its link to Paris, your bubble keeps going for at most 30 seconds, then you see "reconnecting" and you resume on Paris.
- If a Godot server in Montreal crashes, its bubbles are restarted on another Montreal server, or on Paris if none is available.
- If the Paris core goes down, it is like today: players connected to Paris are disconnected, those on other sites hold for at most 30 seconds, and the universe restarts from the save.

## 🗺️ The steps

1. **Measure**: the client measures its ping to test servers in each region. We will know how many players would really benefit before investing.
2. **Edges**: connections go through the closest site, but everything is still simulated in Paris.
3. **Fixed delegation**: a fixed bubble (a planet, for example) is handed to Montreal by configuration, to test switches and outages.
4. **Dynamic delegation**: computing the best site and switching automatically.
5. **Dynamic bubbles**: bubbles that follow the players, moving border and pre-emptive merge. This is where most of the gain is.
6. **Resilience and polish**: direct link between Montreal and Hong Kong, backup core, etc.

Alongside SMD-G, another lead is planned: sending you your own position as soon as it is computed, without waiting for the next batched send. That could save up to 20 or 25 ms **for every player**, even in Paris.
